How this map is calculated
  • A scheduled script, not your browser, retrieves the data and does the calculations: recent readings from ECCC’s real-time hydrometric service, and history from ECCC’s approved national archive (HYDAT). The map only reads the resulting files.
  • The user can choose between two precomputed historical distributions: same calendar date or a seasonal window around that date (daily means from a range of days centred on the comparison date across usable historical years).
  • The recent value is a rolling 24-hour time-weighted mean ending at the latest reading, and needs at least 18 hours of readings. The 24-hour minimum and maximum are shown under “Last 24 hours” without a percentile, because the archive holds daily means only.
  • The percentile ranks the 24-hour mean against every past daily mean in the comparison. The median and the middle half of past values (25th to 75th percentile) come from the same daily means, in the measurement’s units, so the size of a difference can be read directly.
  • “Higher than” or “lower than” a number of past years compares the 24-hour mean with one value per past year: that year’s daily mean on the comparison date, or, for the seasonal window, the average of that year’s daily means in the window. With the seasonal window, this count and the percentile can differ slightly.
  • A percentile needs at least 10 years of record for that station and comparison method. For water level, past years on a clearly different datum (the gauge’s reference level) from today’s reading are left out; smaller datum changes are not yet detected, so water-level ranks are less certain than flow ranks. Readings more than 48 hours old are treated as out of date and not compared.
  • Ice: where ECCC flags ice on a station’s readings in the latest 24 hours, neither its flow nor its water level is compared, because ice can push levels up and makes flow estimates uncertain. ECCC flags ice on flow only, so at stations that measure only water level, winter ranks may read high.
  • Where a station measures both, its water-level rank is checked against its flow rank over the same years. If they differ by 25 points or more, the level is shown without a rank: that usually means the gauge or the river channel has changed, and flow, which ECCC corrects for such changes, is the better guide.
  • All available years are used, including years before dams, reservoirs or diversions changed a river. Where a river is regulated, today’s level or flow can reflect how it is managed, so an unusual rank does not by itself mean unusual weather.
  • Percentile colour belongs to the station point only. Watershed polygons remain neutral geographic context and are never coloured from a single station measurement.
  • Temperature appears only where a hydrometric station publishes a direct sensor channel, and without a percentile, because no historical temperature record is available. No weather-station substitution or modelled water temperature is used.
Data: Environment and Climate Change Canada real-time hydrometric data (provisional and subject to revision) and the HYDAT national archive. Published-measurement statuses come from a dated check of ECCC and WSA sources. Map geometry: simplified from official Saskatchewan Water Security Agency province-boundary and watershed exports.
